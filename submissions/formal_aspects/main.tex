\begin{abstract}
This paper defines a small strong-Kleene status algebra, a typed boundary
between assumptions and checked results, and a cross-prover regression suite.
The contribution is specification discipline, not a solution to an open
mathematical problem.
\end{abstract}
\section{Problem}
Exploratory computational models can silently promote assumptions and sampled
evidence to universal claims. We encode status and trust boundaries explicitly.
\section{Semantics}
Let $K_3=\{F,U,T\}$, with negation fixing $U$, conjunction given by minimum and
disjunction by maximum in the information order $F<U<T$. The implementation
proves involution and commutativity by exhaustive finite cases.
\section{Refinement interface}
Generated statements carry one of: definition, assumption, open obligation,
tested invariant, or checker-verified theorem. No exporter changes status
without a verifier record containing tool, version and digest.
\section{Case study}
A rational accumulator and isolation operator illustrate how domain hypotheses
are made explicit. Monotonicity requires non-negative time increments; reducing
one coordinate is not interpreted as a theorem about topology or security.
\section{Evaluation plan}
The camera-ready paper requires successful logs from pinned Lean, Coq, Isabelle
and Agda versions, mutation tests for the exporters, and a comparison with
existing proof-document exchange formats.
\section{Threats to validity}
The present cross-language fragments are small and do not establish semantic
equivalence between proof assistants. Numerical examples do not validate the
chosen scientific interpretation.
